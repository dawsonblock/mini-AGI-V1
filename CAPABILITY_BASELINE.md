# Campaign 3B.3 — Priority 2: cost, quality and reliability baselines

> **Superseded scoring (3B.3, RD-18).** Accuracy numbers in this
> document were produced by `exact-match-v1` (case-insensitive), which
> accepted wrong-case outputs on case-transformation tasks. Treat every
> accuracy/delta below as **provisional**; strict `exact-match-v2`
> rescores live in `results/campaign3b2/RESCORE_SUMMARY.json`, each
> run's `RESCORE-STRICT.json`, and `CAMPAIGN3B_RESCORE_REPORT.md`.
> Corrections in brief: frozen hidden 0.417 -> 0.333; L5b replay
> advantage = one net row (p = 1.0), not two; rank-ladder magnitudes
> are additionally confounded by alpha/r covariation (RD-13).

Status: **measured from preserved evidence.** Generated by
`scripts/analysis/capability_baseline.py`; machine-readable result in
`CAPABILITY_BASELINE.json`.

"Independently measurable intelligence" needs three numbers per arm, not
one: what it scored, what it cost, and how much the score moves when you
run it again. A quality figure alone cannot separate an arm that is
better from one that is luckier, and none of them answers whether an
improvement "justifies its complete cost".

## Quality — from the campaign evidence

Frozen baseline **0.417** on the 12 held-out tasks.

| arm | class | accuracy | vs frozen | destroyed | gained |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L5b** | **memory** (frozen weights) | **0.583** | **+0.167** | **0** | **2** |
| L1 | frozen | 0.417 | 0.000 | 0 | 0 |
| L5 | memory, self-verified | 0.417 | 0.000 | 0 | 0 |
| NC | control (shuffled labels) | 0.250 | −0.167 | 2 | 0 |
| L6 | LoRA | 0.167 | −0.250 | 3 | 0 |
| L7 | LoRA + replay | 0.083 | −0.333 | 4 | 0 |

**The only arm that beats the frozen baseline is the non-neural one.** The
verified-replay memory arm gains 2 tasks and destroys **none**. Every LoRA
arm scores *below* frozen and destroys 3–4 of the 5 answers frozen got
right. The shuffled-label control sits between them, which is the tell:
the LoRA arms are not merely unhelpful, they are near the floor a
deliberately deranged supervision produces.

## Reliability — from the fit evidence

The campaign evidence is a single seed and **cannot support a variance
claim at all**, so reliability is computed from the multi-seed fit
evidence and the campaign is reported NOT_ESTIMABLE rather than given a
spread it has not earned.

13 multi-seed cells. Frozen baseline 0.417 (v1 corpus) / 0.400 (v2).

| corpus | families | rank | seeds | frozen | adapted | delta | sd | seeds >0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | 1 | 16 | 12 | 0.417 | 0.312 | −0.104 | **0.198** | 3/12 |
| v1 | 2 | 16 | 19 | 0.417 | 0.377 | −0.039 | **0.174** | 9/19 |
| v1 | 4 | 16 | 8 | 0.417 | 0.323 | −0.094 | **0.181** | 3/8 |
| v1 | 5 | 16 | 3 | 0.417 | 0.417 | −0.000 | 0.144 | 1/3 |
| v1 | 6 | 16 | 4 | 0.417 | 0.250 | −0.167 | 0.000 | 0/4 |
| v2 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 0.400 | 0.277 | −0.123 | 0.135 | 1/5 |
| v2 | 2 | 8 | 3 | 0.400 | 0.397 | −0.003 | 0.029 | 1/3 |
| **v2** | **2** | **16** | **4** | 0.400 | 0.406 | **+0.006** | 0.012 | 1/4 |
| v2 | 2 | 32 | 5 | 0.400 | 0.288 | −0.112 | 0.126 | 0/5 |
| v2 | 6 | 4 | 3 | 0.400 | 0.294 | −0.106 | 0.069 | 0/3 |
| v2 | 6 | 8 | 3 | 0.400 | 0.250 | −0.150 | 0.055 | 0/3 |
| v2 | 6 | 16 | 3 | 0.400 | 0.242 | −0.158 | 0.067 | 0/3 |
| v2 | 6 | 32 | 3 | 0.400 | 0.261 | −0.139 | 0.035 | 0/3 |

**Twelve of thirteen cells are negative. The one positive cell is +0.006
with sd 0.012.**

The more important number is the ratio: **the seed-to-seed spread is two
to four times the effect size** in every v1 cell (sd 0.174–0.198 against
deltas of −0.04 to −0.17). The adaptation effect is not merely small; it
is **indistinguishable from seed noise**, and what signal exists points
downward.

(The v2 destroyed/gained counts are larger in absolute terms because v2
has 120 evaluation tasks against v1's 12. The counts are not comparable
across corpora; the rates are.)

## Improvement per unit cost

`delta_mean / mean_train_seconds`, over the estimated cells:

| corpus | families | rank | delta | train s | delta per second |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | 1 | 16 | −0.104 | 99.7 | −0.001045 |
| v1 | 2 | 16 | −0.039 | 121.8 | −0.000324 |
| v1 | 4 | 16 | −0.094 | 150.2 | −0.000624 |
| v1 | 5 | 16 | −0.000 | 99.5 | −0.000000 |
| v1 | 6 | 16 | −0.167 | 280.3 | −0.000595 |
| v2 | 2 | 4 | −0.123 | 88.3 | −0.001397 |

**Every value is negative.** Adaptation buys a worse score for real
compute. The one arm with a positive delta — verified replay — costs
**zero training seconds**, because it changes no weights.

## What this says about the three questions

| question | answer from the evidence |
|---|---|
| **1. What new capability was acquired?** | On the held-out tasks, **none by adaptation**. The memory arm acquires 2 tasks' worth of correct answers over frozen; the LoRA arms acquire nothing and lose 3–4. |
| **2. Which intervention caused the improvement?** | **Verified replay** — non-parametric, frozen weights, no training. Not LoRA, at any rank or breadth measured. |
| **3. Did it transfer, persist, and justify its cost?** | The memory arm transfers (+0.167), persists (0 destroyed), and costs nothing. The LoRA arms transfer negatively, destroy capability, and cost 88–280 s per run. **There is no improvement to justify.** |

## The honest boundary

This is a **diagnostic** result on one small corpus family, one model, and
a 12–120 task evaluation. It does not establish that LoRA cannot help on
other tasks, and it does not establish that the memory arm generalises
beyond lexically retrievable transformations — the memory arm is
*retrieval*, and the held-out families share surface structure with the
training families, which is exactly the condition under which retrieval
should win.

What it does establish, with cost and reliability attached, is that
**within this benchmark the non-neural baseline dominates**, and that any
future claim of neural improvement has to clear a floor (the shuffled
control) *and* a seed spread two to four times larger than the effect it
is claiming.
