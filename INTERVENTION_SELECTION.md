# Campaign 3B.3 — Priority 3: evidence-driven intervention selection

Status: **implemented and executed.** Generated by
`scripts/analysis/intervention_selection.py`; machine-readable result in
`INTERVENTION_SELECTION.json`.

Priority 3 asks to "start with frozen-weight memory, advance to
controlled LoRA, then implement evidence-driven intervention selection".
The first two exist and are measured (`CAPABILITY_BASELINE.md`). This is
the third: a selector that chooses the next intervention from what the
measurements say, and — the part that matters — **refuses a lever the
evidence has already refuted.**

That refusal is the entire difference between an evidence-driven selector
and a fixed script.

## Measured lever effects

Every number is computed from the evidence files. Nothing is a constant.

| lever | measured spread | against seed sd | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **preservation** | **0.700** | — | `CAPABILITY_BASELINE.json` |
| **composition** | **0.389** | — | `family-composition.json` |
| optimisation (steps) | 0.125 | 0.059 | `contrast-summary.json` |
| capacity (rank) | 0.053 | **0.047** | `rank-ladder.json` |

`preservation` is the mean fraction of frozen-correct answers the LoRA
arms destroy. `composition` is the spread across family pairs at fixed
breadth, examples, steps and seeds.

## The criterion, and why it is not a constant

Flatness is **not** tested against a magic threshold. It is tested
against the noise the measurement carries: **a spread across levels that
does not exceed the seed-to-seed sd is not a finding.** The tool reports
`spread <= seed sd` as its reason.

This matters because a fixed threshold would have decided the capacity
question by 0.003: rank's spread is 0.0528 against a sd of 0.0472 — a
ratio of 1.12. That is marginal, not decisive, and a threshold at 0.05
would have called it flat for no principled reason.

## The direction test — what actually refuses the capacity candidate

Magnitude alone does not refuse `E5_rank_or_target_modules`. The
**direction** does. The candidate assumes *more capacity helps*; the
sweep measured the opposite ordering:

```
6 families, rank means:  4 -> 0.294   8 -> 0.250   16 -> 0.242   32 -> 0.261
```

**Rank 4 is the best of the four, and the ordering is not monotone.**
So the candidate is refused with its own assumption quoted, regardless of
how large the spread is. A lever that moves the wrong way is not a basis
for the remedy built on it.

## Result

| candidate | lever | status | measured effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E2 replay during training** | preservation | **ELIGIBLE** | **0.700** |
| **E3 frozen-reference regularization** | preservation | **ELIGIBLE** | **0.700** |
| E1 balanced sampling | composition | ELIGIBLE | 0.389 |
| E6 task-conditioned adapters | composition | ELIGIBLE | 0.389 |
| E4 lower learning rate | optimisation | ELIGIBLE | 0.125 |
| **E5 rank or target modules** | capacity | **REFUSED-DIRECTION** | 0.053 |

**Recommendation: `E2_replay_during_training`.**

The two preservation-targeted candidates rank first because capability
destruction is the largest measured effect in the campaign — the LoRA
arms destroy 70% of the answers frozen got right. The two
composition-targeted candidates follow, because composition is the
largest measured *design* lever. `E5` is refused; a fixed script built
from the plan's candidate list would have picked it.

`E4` stays eligible rather than refused, which is deliberate: the rank
ladder varied rank at **one** learning rate and **one** schedule, so a
rank × learning-rate interaction is not excluded, and the optimisation
lever has a real (if modest) measured spread of 0.125 against a sd of
0.059.

## What this is and is not

**Is:** a mechanism that turns measurements into a ranked, justified
selection, and that cannot recommend a remedy the evidence contradicts.
It reads five evidence artifacts, and if they are absent it refuses to
select at all rather than falling back to the candidate list.

**Is not:** a claim that the recommended intervention will work. The
selector ranks by *measured basis*, not by predicted outcome. `E2` is
recommended because preservation is where the evidence shows the damage,
not because replay-during-training is known to fix it.
